# 10. Past winners are the floor. The 2026 bar is much higher.

Written 2026-08-31. This file exists to stop a specific failure: treating the winner anatomies in `08-winner-patterns.md` as a target rather than a baseline.

## 1. The correction

`08-winner-patterns.md` catalogues what won SIH between 2022 and 2025. Every one of those findings remains true as history. **None of them should be read as a standard to hit.**

The clearest evidence is the closest analogue we found. NTRO's SIH1447 in 2023 asked for identification of forward error correction schemes in unknown signals. The winning team took Rs 1,00,000 with a Flask application wrapping three Keras classifiers, in which the operator selects the modulation from a dropdown, so the blind part of a blind-identification problem was manual input. Input bits were read as characters and fed raw to a network with no signal processing at all. Its three model-loading branches are mislabelled against their own filenames. Its single accuracy figure, 92 percent, exists only as part of a filename, with no test set, no SNR sweep and no confusion matrix. That artifact won a national final two and a half years ago.

In 2026, with every team AI-assisted, that entry does not survive national screening.

## 2. What AI inflation does to the field

The reasonable assumption for SIH 2026:

- **The median entry rises sharply.** Working full-stack applications, clean dashboards, plausible architecture diagrams and confident write-ups are now available to any team that can prompt. What used to distinguish the top decile is becoming table stakes.
- **The variance at the top compresses.** More teams will arrive with something that runs.
- **Polish stops being a differentiator.** If everyone's interface is good, a good interface earns nothing. It only avoids a penalty, since three independent winner accounts say the most common jury feedback is about the demo surface.

So the question that decides SIH 2026 is: **what does AI assistance not lift?**

## 3. What AI does not lift, and where we compete

1. **Physical correctness.** A language model will produce turbulence that looks convincing and is not a Kolmogorov or von Karman phase screen. It will produce noise that is visually plausible and statistically wrong. An SAC optics engineer distinguishes these immediately. Correctness of the underlying physics is checkable, is not generatable, and is exactly where a team that actually understands the model wins.
2. **Performance on someone else's data.** Benchmark Performance-2 is 30 percent of the marks for PS 26169 and is scored on .mp4 files ISRO supplies, against error values ISRO has already fixed. No amount of AI assistance fakes a number on data you have never seen. **In an AI-saturated year, externally anchored scoring is the most valuable property a problem statement can have**, and this is the strongest single reason the pick is 26169 rather than anything scored on self-generated evidence.
3. **Engineering judgment under a real constraint.** A control loop that stays stable under actuator slew limits and latency is not a prompt result. It is tuning, instrumentation and understanding.
4. **Honest measurement.** Reporting where your method fails, and reporting a classical baseline that sometimes beats your learned one, is a signal no team optimising for appearance will send. It is also the thing that survives a hostile question in a 3-minute Q and A.
5. **Verification rigour.** Coverage closure, constrained-random testing and pre-registered analysis are practices, not artifacts. Almost no student team brings them. This team demonstrably does.

## 4. What "ten times the past winner" means concretely for PS 26169

The past-winner standard was: a working demo plus a market slide. The standard we set instead, mapped to ISRO's own published marking scheme:

**Functional Verification, 20 percent.** A standalone executable that runs on a judge's machine with no setup. Every mandatory function present, including all four required target motions (straight line, circular, figure of eight, random) and the optional ones. A GUI that exposes every parameter in the annexure table as a live control, so a judge can pose a scenario and watch the system answer it.

**Benchmark Performance-1, 30 percent.** Automatic performance logging built from commit one, not bolted on. Every run emits simulation duration, FPS, acquisition time, average and maximum tracking error, lock retention rate and processing time without being asked.

**Benchmark Performance-2, 30 percent.** External video ingestion as a first-class input path, architected on day one as a swappable frame source. This is the requirement most teams will miss entirely because it is only visible in the annexure, and it cannot be retrofitted in 32 hours.

**Technical Evaluation, 20 percent.** The report carries what an optics engineer looks for: turbulence characterised by the refractive index structure constant and the Fried parameter rather than by an arbitrary noise slider; a stated actuator model with slew rate and latency; a classical centroid plus Kalman baseline reported as a headline result alongside any learned method, including the regimes where the classical method wins; and a failure analysis naming the conditions under which lock is lost.

**Beyond the rubric, the things that make it memorable.** A measured curve of lock retention against turbulence strength, so the panel sees where the system breaks rather than only that it works. Reported margin against each of the five published targets, not a bare pass. And the honest negative result kept in the report rather than deleted, which is this team's documented signature and is precisely the thing an AI-drafted entry will never contain.

## 5. Assume a strong competitor exists

The right posture is to assume an IIT team with a photonics lab and a publishing advisor is entering this problem statement. ISRO problem statements attract that tier: SIH1740 in 2024 was won by IIT Guwahati.

Our disadvantage against them is optics domain depth. Our advantages are specific and real: hardware signal-acquisition experience where beacon acquisition over a field of view is structurally a correlation search; Monte Carlo simulation of physical systems; constrained-random verification with coverage closure, which maps directly onto proving the tracker across the full parameter space in the annexure table rather than at a few sample points; and pre-registered statistical discipline, which is how you report a tracking-error distribution honestly instead of quoting a best run.

A red-team exercise modelling exactly that competitor is running now, and its output should be treated as the specification to beat.

## 6. The standing instruction

Do not benchmark against 2023. Benchmark against the strongest entry a well-resourced, AI-assisted team could produce in eight weeks, and then find the axis where that entry is still weak. On this problem statement that axis is physical correctness, external-benchmark performance and honest measurement, in that order.
